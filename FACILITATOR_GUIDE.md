# Facilitator guide — THE ETHICS PROTOCOL: Every Decision Has a Consequence (online)

**Goal:** participants can recognize an ethical risk in an AI decision and choose a responsible next step: notice unintended bias, protect affected people, escalate to the people empowered to act, keep humans in the loop and explain their reasoning.
**Format:** individual, 10–12 minutes of play + 5–7 minutes of debrief. (Classroom version: groups of 3–4 discuss about 5 minutes — a group can play on one screen and argue each choice.)

## Know the source before you present
- From the course notes: the situation, the two questions (*What would you do next? Who would you tell about this?*), and the tip **“there is no single ‘correct’ answer to most ethics dilemmas — the goal is the discussion; let groups disagree openly.”** The handout is not in the deck.
- The story (company, people, numbers) is **fictional**. The game scores transparent criteria; it is not a claim that one path is the only right one. Use the ANSWER KEY module to explain the reasoning behind each option.

## Before the session (5 min)
- Open the link → Trainer (PIN 2026). One session per group (**+ New session**). Put the **player link** (or the 5-character code) in the chat. Optional: read the **ANSWER KEY (trainer only)** in the dashboard.

## What happens (for narration)
1. **The discovery** — a recruiter finds a pattern and asks what you will do next.
2. **The deadline** — a hiring manager needs 12 hires by Friday; his reaction depends on what you did in scene 1.
3. **Who needs to know?** — each person has a real responsibility; tap in the order you would brief them.
4. **Protecting the candidates** — corrective measures; some need people you must have told; no quick fix guarantees fairness.
5. **The consequences** — one of five endings, shaped by the whole sequence, a decision timeline and a reflection.

## The reasoning behind the options (also in the dashboard)
- **Scene 1:** A (gather evidence + hold affected rejections) and B (escalate now) take the signal seriously; B informs the owner and governance early but lacks evidence. C (keep using) and D (“not intentional”) let harm continue — intent is not the test. E (adjust scores yourself) hides the symptom, is unreviewed and leaves no trail.
- **Scene 2:** A (human review of *all* automated rejections + an interim plan) protects everyone near the cut-off and keeps the business moving. B protects one group only. C (“investigate after Friday”) keeps the flaw deciding. D (pause with no plan) ignores the business. E hands accountability to someone who can’t fix the flaw.
- **Scene 3:** HR leadership (accountable for fair hiring), the AI system owner (the only one who can investigate and fix the model) and governance (independent oversight, audit trail) are the core; legal & privacy is appropriate here (possible discrimination, use of applicants’ data), usually via HR or governance. The hiring manager is already involved; a company-wide chat or contacting candidates alone is premature; “tell nobody until I’m sure” means the people who could fix it never hear.
- **Scene 4:** responsible measures — review affected decisions, a standing human-review step, investigate the model and data, document, monitor after changes, a review path for candidates through HR and legal. Superficial: remove university names and call it solved; hand-add points; wait for a complaint. A measure that needs someone you did not tell is weaker.
- **Endings:** responsible intervention · contained but incomplete · delayed response with preventable harm · superficial fix · silence. Early harm cannot be erased by a strong finish: sequence matters.

## Debrief questions
1. *(Course notes)* Was it easy or hard to agree as a group? Why?
2. *(Course notes)* What is your own company’s process for reporting a problem like this?
3. *(Suggested)* What did the deadline do to your thinking? Which decision shaped your ending the most?
4. *(Suggested)* Who in your organization would be your “Youssef”, your “Mona” and your “Dr. Salma”? Who is missing?
5. *(Suggested)* The game scores criteria, not a single right answer — where would you disagree with the scoring, and why?

## Notes
- Let people disagree openly; the ANSWER KEY explains the reasoning, it is not meant to prove anyone wrong.
- Scoring never rewards the most dramatic or the fastest option. Use it for feedback, not for grading people.
- If the live connection drops: **Refresh** on the dashboard rebuilds from the relay history; otherwise collect the downloaded reports and use **Import**. Export CSV at the end if you want the results.
